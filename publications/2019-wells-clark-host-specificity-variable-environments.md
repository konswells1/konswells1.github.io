# Abstract
# =====

# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  Host specificity encompasses the range and diversity of host species that a parasite is capable of infecting and is considered a crucial measure of a parasite’s potential to shift hosts and trigger disease emergence. Yet empirical studies rarely consider that regional observations only reflect a parasite’s ‘realized’ host range under particular conditions: the true ‘fundamental’ range of host specificity is typically not approached. We provide an overview of challenges and directions in modelling host specificity under variable environmental conditions. Combining tractable modelling frameworks with multiple data sources that account for the strong interplay between a parasite's evolutionary history, transmission mode, and environmental filters that shape host–parasite interactions will improve efforts to quantify emerging disease risk in times of global change.

summary: >
  This review develops a conceptual and quantitative framework for
  understanding host specificity as a dynamic property emerging from the
  interaction between parasite evolution, host community composition and
  environmental conditions. It argues that observed host ranges represent
  realized host specificity rather than the broader fundamental host range,
  and reviews modelling approaches capable of improving prediction of host
  shifting and emerging infectious diseases.

knowledge_summary: >
  The paper reframes host specificity from a static descriptive property to a
  dynamic eco-evolutionary process shaped jointly by evolutionary history,
  ecological opportunity and environmental filtering. By integrating niche
  theory, community ecology and epidemiological modelling, it establishes a
  theoretical foundation for distinguishing realized from fundamental host
  specificity and outlines how probabilistic, trait-based and network
  modelling approaches can improve forecasting of parasite spillover under
  environmental change.

impact_statement: >
  By demonstrating that host specificity varies across environmental
  gradients and should not be regarded as a fixed species trait, the review
  provides an important conceptual basis for improving prediction of host
  shifting, zoonotic emergence and disease risk in a rapidly changing world.

plain_language_summary: >
  A parasite does not always infect every species that it is capable of
  infecting. Which hosts become infected depends not only on evolution but
  also on local environmental conditions, climate and which host species are
  present. This review explains why observations from one place or time may
  underestimate a parasite's true host range and describes modern modelling
  approaches that can better predict when diseases may spread into new host
  species, including humans.
