# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  The relationship of different types of grassland use with plant species richness and composition (functional groups of herbs, legumes, and grasses) has so far been studied at small regional scales or comprising only few components of land use. We comprehensively studied the relationship between abandonment, fertilization, mowing intensity, and grazing by different livestock types on plant diversity and composition of 1514 grassland sites in three regions in North-East, Central and South-West Germany. We further considered environmental site conditions including soil type and topographical situation. Fertilized grasslands showed clearly reduced plant species diversity (−15% plant species richness, −0.1 Shannon diversity on fertilized grassland plots of 16 m2) and changed composition (−3% proportion of herb species), grazing had the second largest effects and mowing the smallest ones. Among the grazed sites, the ones grazed by sheep had higher than average species richness (+27%), and the cattle-grazed ones lower (−42%). Further, these general results were strongly modulated by interactions between the different components of land use and by regional context: land-use effects differed largely in size and sometimes even in direction between regions. This highlights the importance of comparing different regions and involving a large number of plots when studying relationships between land use and plant diversity. Overall, our results show that great caution is necessary when extrapolating results and management recommendations to other regions.

  This empirical study evaluated abandonment, fertilization, mowing and grazing
  effects on vascular plant diversity and functional-group composition across
  1,514 grassland plots in the three German Biodiversity Exploratories.

# Knowledge-network summary.
knowledge_summary: >
  The publication demonstrates that fertilization, mowing and grazing do not
  operate as independent, spatially uniform drivers of grassland plant
  diversity. Fertilization produced the strongest overall reductions in
  richness, grazing effects depended strongly on livestock type, and all major
  responses were modified by interactions with land-use type and region.
  Environmental covariates further explained substantial variation among
  sites.

# Scientific or societal significance.
impact_statement: >
  Effective grassland biodiversity management requires region-specific
  strategies that account for interactions among fertilization, mowing,
  grazing, livestock type and environmental conditions.

# Non-technical summary.
plain_language_summary: >-
  Managing grasslands affects plant diversity in different ways depending on
  where and how the land is used. Fertilized grasslands generally supported
  fewer plant species, sheep grazing was associated with higher richness and
  cattle grazing with lower richness. However, these patterns varied greatly
  among German regions, so management advice developed in one region may not
  work elsewhere.
