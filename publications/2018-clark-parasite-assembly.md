## Why this paper matters
Parasite communities are not assembled randomly. They are shaped by the ecological and evolutionary structure of host communities, by climatic conditions and by the degree to which host communities are connected across regions.
This paper helps explain why parasites establish in some places but not others, and why changing host distributions may alter future patterns of wildlife disease and parasite invasion.

## Key findings
- Host phylogenetic distinctiveness contributed to regional parasite assembly.
- Parasite turnover was shaped by host turnover, climate similarity and geographic distance.
- More connected host communities may provide greater opportunities for parasite establishment and spread.
- Changes in host distributions could weaken existing barriers to parasite invasion.

## Suggested teaching use
This paper can be used to discuss how ecological communities assemble across regions, how host phylogeny influences parasite diversity, and how statistical models can separate climatic, geographic and biological drivers of community structure.
