abstract_original: |-
  Protecting habitat and the species they shelter by setting up protected areas (PAs) has become a conservation priority to mitigate the current extinction crisis. This strategy has improved different aspects of biodiversity including species richness and abundance across ecosystems. However, to truly understand the effectiveness of mitigation measures against global environmental change, we must account for one of the fundamental dimensions of biodiversity: species interactions. Using 376 556 curated citizen science records of 509 bird species distributed across 45 networks of European PAs (collections of connected PAs), we show that the effects of PAs on the structure of avian food webs are mixed across Europe. Overall effects of protection include an increase in species richness and larger body masses of both top and intermediate species. For other food web features, the sign and magnitude of the effects are mixed. Our results further suggest that these effects are strongly influenced by geographical and environmental features of the PA networks such as remoteness, habitat diversity, human pressure and agriculture. Lastly, PAs with specific protection goals such as those administered by European Bird Directives for conservation sustain more complex food webs. Our study provides evidence for the need of including clear management goals and considering environmental context in the designation of PAs to increase their effectiveness at preserving biodiversity beyond species richness.

summary: >
  This study evaluated how protected areas influence avian food-web structure
  across 45 protected-area networks in Europe using 376,556 curated citizen
  science records representing 509 bird species. Local food webs were
  reconstructed from species co-occurrence and a continental tetrapod trophic
  metaweb, and 16 network and body-size metrics were compared between protected
  and nearby non-protected areas. Protected areas were associated overall with
  higher species richness and larger intermediate and top species, but most
  other food-web responses varied among networks. Protection outcomes depended
  strongly on management goals, habitat diversity, remoteness, topography,
  agriculture, urbanisation and human pressure.

knowledge_summary: >
  The publication advances protected-area assessment beyond species counts by
  analysing ecological interaction networks at continental scale. It shows that
  protected areas do not produce a single consistent food-web response:
  protection can support larger predators, longer food chains and more complex
  trophic structure in some contexts, while effects on many other metrics are
  neutral or heterogeneous. The results identify landscape context and
  designation purpose as important mediators of protection outcomes and
  demonstrate that ecological-network metrics reveal conservation effects that
  species richness alone cannot capture.

impact_statement: >
  Protected areas can conserve important features of avian trophic networks,
  but their outcomes depend on landscape setting, anthropogenic pressure and
  clearly defined management goals. Conservation evaluations should therefore
  include ecological interactions alongside species richness.

plain_language_summary: >
  Protected areas are usually judged by how many species they contain, but
  healthy ecosystems also depend on how species interact. This study compared
  bird food webs inside and outside protected areas across Europe. Protected
  areas generally contained more bird species and larger predators, but their
  effects on other aspects of food-web structure differed greatly among
  locations. Areas managed specifically for birds and those with favourable
  environmental conditions tended to support more complex food webs.
