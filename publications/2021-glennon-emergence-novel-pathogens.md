abstract_original: |-
  The emergence of infectious agents with pandemic potential present scientific challenges from detection to data interpretation to understanding determinants of risk and forecasts. Mathematical models could play an essential role in how we prepare for future emergent pathogens. Here, we describe core directions for expansion of the existing tools and knowledge base, including: using mathematical models to identify critical directions and paths for strengthening data collection to detect and respond to outbreaks of novel pathogens; expanding basic theory to identify infectious agents and contexts that present the greatest risks, over both the short and longer term; by strengthening estimation tools that make the most use of the likely range and uncertainties in existing data; and by ensuring modelling applications are carefully communicated and developed within diverse and equitable collaborations for increased public health benefit.

summary: >
  This topical review identifies major challenges in modelling the emergence
  of novel pathogens, from surveillance and data integration to predicting
  zoonotic potential, estimating early epidemic parameters and translating
  models into public-health action. It argues that mathematical models can
  guide data collection, strengthen inference under uncertainty and improve
  preparedness when embedded in transparent, equitable and interdisciplinary
  collaborations.

knowledge_summary: >
  The paper frames pathogen emergence as a multi-stage process involving
  introduction into a new host population and subsequent establishment of
  sustained transmission. It connects surveillance design, pathogen and host
  traits, immune landscapes, evolutionary change, within-host processes,
  population heterogeneity, parameter estimation and health-system capacity.
  The review also treats modelling as a social and institutional practice:
  data biases, structural inequalities, communication failures and unequal
  access to analytical capacity can shape both scientific inference and public
  health outcomes.

impact_statement: >
  The review provides a research agenda for building modelling systems capable
  of detecting and interpreting novel pathogen emergence while supporting
  responsible public-health decisions. Its integration of biological theory,
  data design, uncertainty, health systems and equity broadens pandemic
  modelling beyond technical forecasting toward a more robust and socially
  accountable preparedness framework.

plain_language_summary: >
  Models can help society detect new pathogens, understand which ones may
  become dangerous and decide how to respond during the uncertain early stages
  of an outbreak. Their usefulness depends on better surveillance, careful use
  of incomplete data, stronger biological understanding and honest
  communication of uncertainty. Effective pandemic preparation also requires
  cooperation across countries and disciplines and attention to inequalities
  in health systems, data availability and access to modelling expertise.
