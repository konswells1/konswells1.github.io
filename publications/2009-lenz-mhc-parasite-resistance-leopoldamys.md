# Abstract

# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  Background: Genes of the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) code for key functions in the adaptive immune response of vertebrates and most of them show exceptionally high polymorphism. This polymorphism has been associated with selection by diverse and changing parasite communities. We analysed MHC class IIB diversity, gastrointestinal parasite load and body condition in the wild-ranging tropical rat Leopoldamys sabanus under natural selection conditions in a highly variable rainforest environment in Borneo to explore the mechanisms that maintain these high levels of genetic polymorphism.
  
  Results: Allelic diversity was determined via SSCP and sequencing, and parasite screening was done through non-invasive faecal egg counts. The detected alleles showed high levels of polymorphism and signatures of balancing selection. More diverse MHC genotypes were associated with better body condition and resistance against helminth infection, and the data also suggested a positive effect of MHC allele divergence within individuals on these parameters.
  
  Conclusion: In accordance with the heterozygote-advantage hypothesis, this study provides evidence for an advantage of more diverse MHC genotypes. More specifically, the negative relationship between individual allele divergence and number of parasite species is consistent with the divergent-allele-advantage hypothesis.
