abstract_original: |-
  Aim. Although mosquitoes can have innate preferences for particular
  blood-meal hosts, their realised feeding patterns on different host species
  can be modified under climate and land-use change, with implications for
  disease spread. Therefore, it is important to understand the niche breadth
  of vectors and the extent to which shifts in feeding patterns can be
  predicted.
  Location. Global.
  Time Period. 2000–2019.
  Major Taxa Studied. Six prominent disease-vectoring mosquitoes:
  Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus, Anopheles funestus, Anopheles gambiae,
  Culex pipiens and Culex quinquefasciatus.
  Methods. Focusing on blood-meal studies that used universal molecular
  methods with broad taxonomic coverage, we compiled evidence from more than
  15,600 blood meals. We estimated mosquito host niche breadth and used
  hierarchical Dirichlet regression models to investigate shifts in feeding
  patterns among different functional and taxonomic groups of host species in
  relation to host and environmental factors.
  Results. We estimated host ranges of 179–321 species for each of the two
  Culex mosquitoes and 26–65 species for Aedes mosquitoes, comprising
  considerably broader host niche breadths than previously anticipated. For
  the two Anopheles species, we estimated host ranges of 7–29 species. We
  found some evidence that shifts in feeding patterns among different host
  functional and taxonomic groups were associated with environmental
  conditions such as temperature and livestock density. However, the
  currently available evidence contains substantial uncertainty, which
  impedes global prediction of shifts in mosquito feeding patterns.
  Main Conclusions. The global meta-analysis provides initial insight into
  shifts in feeding patterns across variable environments, suggesting that
  host choice is not a simple function of host availability but is contingent
  on other environmental drivers. Improving the resolution and consistency
  of data collection and reporting will improve the precision with which
  blood-meal studies can inform assessments of present and future pathogen
  transmission risk.

# High-level scientific scope/limitations of the study
scope_and_limitations:
  - Global blood-meal sampling remains geographically uneven.
  - Sampling effort and reporting standards differ substantially among studies.
  - Environmental predictors explain only part of the observed variation in realised host use.
  - Current datasets remain too sparse for robust prediction of feeding shifts across all environmental gradients.
  - Molecular protocols and taxonomic resolution vary among source studies.

# Summaries
# =======

summary: >
  Using more than 15,600 mosquito blood meals compiled from 88 studies
  worldwide, this study shows that common mosquito vectors use substantially
  broader vertebrate host communities than previously recognised and that
  realised feeding patterns vary with environmental conditions.

knowledge_summary: >
  The study reframes mosquito host selection as a dynamic ecological
  interaction rather than a fixed species trait. By combining molecular
  ecology, global evidence synthesis and Bayesian hierarchical modelling, it
  shows that realised host use emerges from interactions among feeding
  tendencies, host availability and environmental context. The work connects
  vector ecology, community ecology and disease ecology while identifying
  major data limitations that constrain predictions of host switching and
  pathogen transmission.

impact_statement: >
  This global synthesis demonstrates that assumptions of narrow or fixed
  mosquito host preferences can underestimate the breadth and flexibility of
  vector-host interactions. Its findings support more dynamic approaches to
  modelling mosquito-borne disease risk under environmental change.
