# Abstract
# =====

# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  Context: Current diversity and species composition cannot always be explained by present land use alone because historical landscape structure may exert long-lasting effects.
  Objectives: The study quantified the effects of present and historical landscape structure on plant and arthropod species richness across temperate grasslands in three German regions.
  Methods: Landscape metrics were calculated from historical and current land-cover maps (1820/50–2016) for 150 grassland plots and analysed using generalized linear models.
  Results: Models combining present and historical landscape metrics best explained several taxonomic and functional groups. Landscape structure from the 1820/50s was frequently retained as a significant predictor.
  Conclusions: Historical landscape structure remains an important determinant of current grassland biodiversity and should be incorporated into conservation planning.

summary: >
  This study investigated how present-day and historical landscape structure
  influence current plant and arthropod species richness across 150 temperate
  grassland plots in the German Biodiversity Exploratories. Using historical
  land-cover maps spanning approximately 200 years (1820/50–2008), landscape
  metrics at multiple spatial scales, and generalized linear models for
  taxonomic and functional groups, the authors demonstrated that historical
  landscape composition and configuration substantially improve explanations of
  present biodiversity patterns beyond current landscape structure and land-use
  intensity alone.

# Purpose: Scientific significance and contribution to knowledge.
knowledge_summary: >
  The study provides strong evidence that historical landscape legacies remain
  major determinants of contemporary grassland biodiversity. It shows that
  ecological responses differ among taxonomic and functional groups, with
  dispersal ability and habitat specialization mediating sensitivity to
  historical landscape change. By demonstrating that biodiversity reflects the
  combined influence of present and past landscape structure over nearly two
  centuries, the study advances understanding of extinction debts, ecological
  time lags and long-term landscape effects in fragmented agricultural
  ecosystems.
