# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  Within host-parasite communities, viral co-circulation and co-infections of hosts are the norm, yet studies of significant emerging zoonoses tend to focus on a single parasite species within the host. Using a multiplexed paramyxovirus bead-based PCR on urine samples from Australian flying foxes, we show that multi-viral shedding from flying fox populations is common. We detected up to nine bat paramyxoviruses shed synchronously. Multi-viral shedding infrequently coalesced into an extreme, brief and spatially restricted shedding pulse, coinciding with peak spillover of Hendra virus, an emerging fatal zoonotic pathogen of high interest. Such extreme pulses of multi-viral shedding could easily be missed during routine surveillance yet have potentially serious consequences for spillover of novel pathogens to humans and domestic animal hosts. We also detected co-occurrence patterns suggestive of the presence of interactions among viruses, such as facilitation and cross-immunity. We propose that multiple viruses may be interacting, influencing the shedding and spillover of zoonotic pathogens. Understanding these interactions in the context of broader scale drivers, such as habitat loss, may help predict shedding pulses of Hendra virus and other fatal zoonoses.

# Concise publication summary.
summary: >
  This empirical study analysed 1,131 pooled urine samples collected from
  Australian flying-fox roosts between 2010 and 2012 using a multiplex
  bead-based PCR assay targeting 11 paramyxoviruses. It combined temporal
  surveillance, Markov random fields and logistic regression to examine
  synchronous shedding, viral co-occurrence and environmental associations.

# Knowledge-network summary.
knowledge_summary: >
  The study shows that bat paramyxovirus communities exhibit strongly
  clustered, multi-virus shedding dynamics. Although average prevalence was
  low, nine viruses were detected and several coalesced into an intense
  winter 2011 shedding pulse that coincided with unprecedented Hendra virus
  spillover. Conditional association models revealed both positive and
  negative virus-pair patterns, while comparisons across sample and roost
  session levels provided hypotheses about facilitation, cross-immunity,
  co-infection and shared environmental forcing.

# Scientific or societal significance.
impact_statement: >
  Bat-virus surveillance and spillover prediction should account for entire
  viral communities and use sufficiently frequent, long-term sampling to
  detect rare but epidemiologically important shedding pulses.

# Non-technical summary.  
plain_language_summary: >-
  Australian flying foxes often shed several viruses at the same time. During
  one short period in winter 2011, many paramyxoviruses were detected together
  near Boonah while Hendra virus spillovers to horses were unusually frequent.
  Because these pulses are brief and uncommon, routine surveillance may miss
  them. Studying multiple viruses together may therefore improve understanding
  and prediction of zoonotic spillover.
