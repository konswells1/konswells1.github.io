# Abstract
# =====

# Purpose: Original published abstract.
abstract_original: |-
  Introduction: In production forests, management can have cascading effects on biodiversity and ecosystem services. Acoustic diversity reflects the diversity of vocalizing animals and has also considerable recreational value for human well-being, but the relationship between acoustic diversity and forest management remains largely unexplored.
  
  Method: We recorded acoustic diversity on forest plots along a gradient of silvicultural management intensity (SMI) in three regions of Germany. We explored the diurnal and seasonal temporal dynamics in acoustic diversity index (ADI) from March to July using generalized additive mixed models (GAMMs). We further investigated the interrelation between acoustic diversity and silvicultural management intensity, forest structural diversity, as well as tree diversity, bird species richness and abundance using structural equation modeling (SEM).
  
  Results: Silvicultural management intensity had significant effects on the temporal dynamics of ADI in May and June from dawn till dusk, but variance explained by SMI was low. We confirmed our hypothesis that ADI was reduced by SMI due to its cascading effects on forest structural diversity and bird species richness and abundance.
  
  Discussion: Acoustic diversity indices can provide valuable insights into how forest management affects the acoustic activity of soniferous communities. We discuss how this can indicate both changes in species diversity as well as their vocal activity. We further address potential implications for forest management.

  This empirical ecoacoustic study analysed recordings from March to July 2016
  across forest plots in the three German Biodiversity Exploratories to test
  how silvicultural management intensity influences daily and seasonal
  acoustic diversity.

# Knowledge-network summary.
knowledge_summary: >
  Generalized additive mixed models identified when management-related
  differences in acoustic diversity emerged through the spring season, while
  structural equation models traced indirect pathways through forest
  structure, tree diversity and bird communities. Acoustic diversity declined
  with increasing management intensity, particularly in May and June, but bird
  abundance and forest structural heterogeneity explained important parts of
  this relationship.

# Scientific or societal significance.
impact_statement: >
  Ecoacoustic monitoring can reveal temporally specific consequences of forest
  management for soniferous communities and can support forest planning that
  balances timber production, biodiversity and recreational soundscape values.

# Non-technical summary.
plain_language_summary: >-
  Forest soundscapes changed strongly through the day and across spring. More
  intensively managed forests generally had lower acoustic diversity because
  management simplified forest structure and supported fewer birds and bird
  individuals. The effects were clearest in May and June. Sound recordings can
  therefore strengthen large-scale biodiversity monitoring, although they
  should be combined with methods that identify individual species.
