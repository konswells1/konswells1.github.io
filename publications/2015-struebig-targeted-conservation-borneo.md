# Abstract

# Purpose: Original published summary.
abstract_original: |-
  Responses of biodiversity to changes in both land cover and climate are recognized but still poorly understood. This poses significant challenges for spatial planning as species could shift, contract, expand, or maintain their range inside or outside protected areas. We examine this problem in Borneo, a global biodiversity hotspot, using spatial prioritization analyses that maximize species conservation under multiple environmental-change forecasts. Climate projections indicate that 11%–36% of Bornean mammal species will lose at least 30% of their habitat by 2080, and suitable ecological conditions will shift upslope for 23%–46%. Deforestation exacerbates this process, increasing the proportion of species facing comparable habitat loss to 30%–49%, a 2-fold increase on historical trends. Accommodating these distributional changes will require conserving land outside existing protected areas, but this may be less than anticipated from models incorporating deforestation alone because some species will colonize high-elevation reserves. Our results demonstrate the increasing importance of upland reserves and that relatively small additions (16,000–28,000 km2) to the current conservation estate could provide substantial benefits to biodiversity facing changes to land cover and climate. On Borneo, much of this land is under forestry jurisdiction, warranting targeted conservation partnerships to safeguard biodiversity in an era of global change.

# Knowledge-network summary.
knowledge_summary: >
  The study integrates species-distribution modelling, deforestation
  projections and minimum-set spatial prioritisation under multiple climate,
  emission and presence-threshold scenarios. Combined environmental change was
  projected to cause major habitat contraction and upslope shifts for many
  primates, carnivores and bats. Priority-area analyses consistently emphasised
  Borneo's interior uplands, connectivity among reserves and land managed under
  forestry rather than protection confined to the existing reserve network.

# Scientific or societal significance.
impact_statement: >
  Safeguarding Borneo's mammal diversity under climate and land-cover change
  requires climate-smart conservation beyond formal protected areas, with
  targeted partnerships across forestry and other managed landscapes.

# Non-technical summary.
plain_language_summary: >-
  Climate change and deforestation are expected to reduce suitable habitat for
  many of Borneo's mammals and push suitable conditions uphill. By modelling 81
  species, the study identified places that could protect mammals now and in
  future decades. Many priority areas are forests managed for timber rather
  than formal reserves, so cooperation with forestry operators, better
  management and improved habitat connections will be crucial.
